Privacy notice

Last updated: November 13, 2026

This notice explains what personal data zennia processes, why, and how you can exercise your rights under Brazil's General Data Protection Law (LGPD, Law 13,709/2018).

Who is responsible

zennia is a free app for learning Japanese, made and run by Marcos Vinícius Brito de Araújo, the controller of your personal data. For any question or request about your data, write to marcos.nevary@gmail.com.

What data is processed

  • Account. Without signing in, zennia creates an anonymous account that only this browser can reach. If you sign in with Google, it also receives your email address, name, photo and Google account ID. The account keeps when it was created and when you last signed in, and each session keeps the IP address and browser it started from.
  • Profile. The name and photo you set in Settings. A photo you upload is stored at a public address: anyone who has that address can see it.
  • Vocabulary. Your words, with their readings, meanings and tags, whether they are active, and when they were added and activated.
  • Practice. For each word and kana, how many answers were right and wrong, when you last answered it, and how well the practice estimates you remember it. With Help improve zennia on, a log of each answer: the item, whether it was right, and when.
  • Settings. Your preferences in the app, such as the theme, the kana you practice and the practice fonts.
  • Pronunciation. The first time a word is played, its audio is made and stored for everyone who plays the same word, without any link to you.
  • In your browser. Your session and a copy of your theme. zennia uses no advertising or tracking cookies and no third-party analytics.
  • Access logs. The hosting and database providers record technical data such as IP address and browser, to run and protect the service.

Why, and on what legal basis

  • To provide the app: keep your account, sync your vocabulary and practice across devices, and show your statistics. Basis: carrying out the service you asked for (LGPD art. 7, V).
  • To improve zennia: study how the app is used, such as the answer log, to make the practice and the rest of the app better. Basis: legitimate interest (art. 7, IX). You can object at any time by turning off Help improve zennia.
  • To keep the service secure and running, with the access logs. Basis: legitimate interest (art. 7, IX).

Your data is never sold or used for advertising, and zennia makes no automated decisions with legal effects on you.

Who processes it

  • Supabase: database, sign-in and file storage.
  • Vercel: hosts the site.
  • Google: sign-in, if you choose it.
  • Microsoft Azure: makes the pronunciation audio. It receives only the Japanese word and its reading, never data about you.

These providers may store data on servers outside Brazil. This international transfer follows LGPD art. 33, under each provider's data protection terms.

How long it is kept

Your data is kept while your account exists. Resetting the statistics erases your results, the practice's estimates and the answer log. Deleting your account erases everything. If you clear your browser's data without having signed in, that anonymous account can no longer be reached, and its data is not linked to you.

Your rights

Under LGPD art. 18, you can ask for confirmation that your data is processed, access to it, correction, anonymization, blocking or deletion, portability, information about who it is shared with, and object to processing based on legitimate interest. Most of it is in Settings → Data:

  • Download your data: access and portability.
  • Delete account: deletion of everything.
  • Help improve zennia: objection to the log.
  • Your words and profile can be corrected in the app at any time.

For anything else, write to marcos.nevary@gmail.com. Requests are answered within 15 days. You can also complain to the National Data Protection Authority (ANPD).

Children

Children under 12 should only use zennia with the consent of a parent or guardian (LGPD art. 14).

Security

Connections are encrypted, and the database only lets each account read and change its own data. If a security incident could put you at relevant risk, you and the ANPD will be told.

Changes

When this notice changes, the date at the top changes with it.
